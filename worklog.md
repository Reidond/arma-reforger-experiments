## 2026-10-02 — MCDU APEX Multicam trousers (Blender, scripted)

- **Agent:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5) + Codex (image generation)
- **Goal:** Realistic, photogrammetry-like model of MCDU APEX trousers in Multicam for Arma Reforger, built in Blender through the MCP server from 18 product photos and the product description.
- **Done:**
  - Addon `mods/EXP_ApexPants` (GUID `C4A331D81B532878`) via `tools/new_mod.py`.
  - `art/EXP_ApexPants/scripts/`: scripted pipeline — trouser shell from the Reforger template body, sewing-pattern UVs, analytic construction details (`apex_features.py`: cargo/slash/welt/back flap pockets, box pleat, long side vent zip, lower-leg zips, knee/cuff velcro straps, 500D knee panels, stretch gusset/yoke/back-knee panels, waistband, belt loops, fly, double topstitching, ripstop, folds), texture baker (`gen_textures.py`, 4K BCR/NMO), displacement, Cycles AO bake, skinning + LODs + item model + FBX export.
  - Camo print swatches generated by Codex (`gpt-6-sol`; default `gpt-6.1-sol` is rejected for a ChatGPT login), colour-matched to the product photos.
  - Mod resources: FBX (worn: skeleton + LOD0–3 skinned; item: flat mesh + `UBX_Item`), 4K TIFs, `.emat`, three prefabs inheriting vanilla `Pants_M88.et`, hand-written `.meta` files with fixed GUIDs.
  - Renders in `art/EXP_ApexPants/renders/`; notes in `docs/notes/clothing-pipeline.md`.
  - `.gitignore`: `/docs/vendor` without trailing slash (worktree symlink), art `build/` and `textures/preview/`, product photos in `art/*/reference/`.
- **Decisions:**
  - Analytic, script-driven detail instead of image-to-3D generation: Hyper3D/Hunyuan/Tripo are disabled in the Blender add-on, and a generated blob would not fit the skeleton or have usable UVs.
  - Kept the body's own topology (QuadriFlow welded the thighs). LOD0 is 17.7k tris; Enfusion has no tri cap, LODs carry distance.
  - Inherit vanilla `Pants_M88.et` to get storage/pocket behaviour without copying it.
  - Product photos are not committed (third-party); Codex camo swatches are.
- **Verified:** Blender 5.2: FBX re-import (224 bones, 4 LODs, material, collider property); posed stride with body shows no tearing or poke-through; Cycles renders front/back/close-ups/worn.
- **Not verified:** Anything in Workbench — FBX/TIF import with the hand-written `.meta` files, `MatPBRBasic` look in-engine, prefab overrides (component IDs taken from the M88 sample), in-game fit with vanilla boots/jackets, hit zones.
- **Next:** Import in Workbench and fix whatever the `.meta`/prefab guesses get wrong; check clipping with vanilla boots and jackets; consider `MatPBRCamo`; better folded item model; cloth game material on `UBX_Item`; flap edges still show the pocket outline faintly.

## 2026-10-02 — MCDU APEX: match the reference photos, real 3D parts

- **Agent:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5) + Codex (image generation, `gpt-6-sol`)
- **Goal:** User: the first model "does not look like the reference" — iterate until it does, using only the 18 product photos, Codex imagegen and our own tools; then: model the 3D details as real triangles instead of normal-map tricks.
- **Done:**
  - `scripts/compare.py`: poses the Reforger skeleton like each photo, frames the camera the same way, white studio light, writes `renders/compare_<photo>.png` (photo | render) — the acceptance test for every change. Also saves the posed mesh (`build/pose_<photo>.npz`).
  - `scripts/photo_project.py`: projects photos 15 (front mannequin), 8 (walking, back-right) and 13 (side close-up) onto the textures through the posed mesh; mapping anchored on silhouettes and seams (fly, side zip); each view owns the panels it faces (hand-over at seams); mirrored copies cover the other leg; exposure matched on the olive stretch fabric. `photo_intrinsics.py` fills gaps and splits photo shading (Retinex + FFT Poisson) into relief for the normal map.
  - `scripts/fabric_synth.py`: ripstop detail tile from a macro photo (grid 5 mm, measured against the 45 mm belt), plus quilting tools. Codex generated `camo_print_ref_{a,b}.png` from photo crops (used where no photo sees the surface).
  - `scripts/surface_samples.py`, `photo_lookup.py`, `surface_chart.py`: surface (leg angle, height) grid drawn onto the photos and flattened charts — used to read where each feature sits.
  - `scripts/apex_parts.py` + `build_parts.py`: **real geometry** for both cargo pockets (bellows bag + box pleat), cargo flaps, back pockets + flaps, welt lips, waistband, 9 belt loops, side vent zips + pulls, knee pad layer, knee straps + tabs, lower zips + pulls, hem cuffs + tabs. Parts are a top surface plus side walls down to the shell (no hidden underside sharing UVs), wall UVs in short strips; `Pants_full` = shell + parts, one 4K atlas (58 % fill, 0.37 mm/texel).
  - Panel layout in `apex_features.py` corrected from the photos (olive knee band with camo insert, inner-thigh stretch gusset, back-knee/thigh stretch, tall back yoke, calf V-gusset).
  - Export: LOD0 39.8k tris (shell 17.7k + parts 22k), LOD1 19.9k, LOD2 9.9k, LOD3 4.8k; skinned, FBX re-exported.
- **Decisions:**
  - Photo projection over synthetic texturing: only real pixels give "scanned" look; parametric pocket/panel bumps removed from displacement in photo mode (the photos' own shading + real part geometry replace them).
  - Parts built analytically in surface coordinates, so positions are editable numbers in `apex_parts.py`, read off `renders/overlay_*.png`.
- **Verified:** compare renders for photos 15, 13, 8 (Blender Cycles); skinned parts deform in the walking pose used for photo 8; UV fill checked numerically.
- **Not verified:** Workbench import; in-game look (MatPBRBasic); LOD switching; performance of a 40k-tri LOD0.
- **Open:** the textures are derived from the shop's product photos — fine for private experiments, **not publishable** without the seller's permission. Inner thighs/back of the left leg come from mirrored or filled data and are softer; flap undersides are open (single-sided); belt loops are solid blocks.
- **Next:** more photo views (1, 2, 16 close-ups) for the remaining soft areas; tune part outlines against overlays; check LOD1–3 visually.
